# 09a. Robot fleet operations: research evidence

Date: 2026-11-21

This note collects public evidence on how real robot and field-service companies run fleet operations, to inform v2 of our fleet-operations app (about 500 home humanoids, 3 Bay Area hubs, crews with vans, charging and battery swaps at hubs, inspections, incidents). Most sources are vendor pages, press releases or press coverage, so each row says what kind of source it is. Anything we could not verify is marked "not publicly documented". Quotes are limited to short phrases; everything else is paraphrased. Information was gathered by web search and page fetches on 2026-11-21 and some pages were only seen through search summaries (marked "summary-level").

## 2. Research questions answered

**How do commercial fleets represent robot health, charging, failures and serviceability?**
Agility's Arc lists uptime, throughput, Mean Time Between Incidents and per-robot status (row 2). Orbit centers on a map, mission status and alerts with per-user severity settings (row 1). Amazon describes docks spread through buildings and a fixed-interval preventive maintenance program (row 14). Figure names a specific component (the forearm) as the top failure point (row 4). Component-level failure taxonomies, battery state-of-health displays and serviceability scores are not publicly documented in the sources we read.

**How do tasks integrate with customer workflows?**
Warehouse systems expose APIs to WMS/WES/MES (row 2) and Orbit offers APIs and work-order generation (row 1). Field-service tools tie jobs to appointments, territories and customer records (rows 16, 17). For homes, the closest public example is 1X, where the owner schedules operator sessions and describes the task in an app (row 5). Customer notification flows for robots in homes are not publicly documented.

**Which events are escalated to humans vs. automated?**
Waymo and Serve both describe robot-initiated requests for human help, with humans advising rather than driving (rows 8, 10). Waymo separates remote advice from physical roadside recovery and from emergency services (rows 10, 11). Agility centralizes alerts and troubleshooting with a path to vendor support (row 2). The exact thresholds that decide automation versus escalation are not publicly documented. Correlated events can overload human queues (row 12).

**How are field recovery, spares, transport and maintenance organized across sites?**
Waymo uses depots with technicians, dispatch, response and support as distinct teams, plus roadside crews (rows 11, 13). Starship uses in-person fleet attendants (row 9). Locus keeps ownership so it can refurbish and move robots between sites (row 15, secondary source). Spare-parts stocking levels, van inventory practice and inter-hub transfer processes for robots are not publicly documented.

**Which telemetry and reliability metrics predict missed jobs?**
No source publishes a validated predictor of missed jobs. What is published: uptime and MTBI (row 2), runtime hours with failure counts withheld (row 4), help-request backlog as a driver of delay (row 12), and environmental effects on battery (row 15). Our own working hypotheses, not evidence: low battery at job start, open help requests, overdue maintenance, repeat failures of the same component, and time since last calibration. We should log all of these and test them against our own missed-job data.

**What differs between controlled industrial sites and unpredictable homes?**
Industrial sites have a known map, shared safety systems, on-site staff and standards written for them (rows 1-3, 19). Homes vary in layout, pets, children, clutter, connectivity and privacy expectations. The public home-robot evidence is thin and mostly from 1X press coverage (rows 5, 6). Consent, privacy, restricted areas and poor-signal operation become first-class needs (rows 5, 17). Public evidence on injury or damage rates for humanoids in homes: not publicly documented.

## 3. Design implications summary

- **Per-robot and fleet health views using uptime and MTBI-style metrics, with component-level failure tags.** Rows 2, 4.
- **Treat charge state and maintenance-due as part of job readiness, not just a display.** Rows 14, 15.
- **Separate work queues by role at each hub: technicians, dispatch, support, recovery.** Rows 10, 11, 13.
- **A robot-initiated help request queue, distinct from alerts, with queue-depth and per-supervisor load shown.** Rows 8, 10, 12.
- **Remote assistance is consent-based, scheduled, logged and visibly indicated, with per-home restricted areas.** Rows 5, 6.
- **Incident workflow with tiers: remote advice, our recovery crew, emergency services; record third-party involvement.** Rows 10, 11.
- **Area-wide event mode: bulk pause, wave-based recall to hubs, degraded operation.** Row 12.
- **Crew mobile app that works offline: pre-cache home record and robot history, queue photos and signatures, show sync state.** Rows 16, 17.
- **Dispatch console with territory filters and in-day re-planning for crews and vans.** Rows 16, 17.
- **Alert routing by severity and role, with per-user preferences, plus an API and SSO from the start.** Rows 1, 2.
- **Post-repair calibration and an inspection record before a robot returns to service; keep records audit-ready.** Rows 4, 18, 19.
- **Log candidate predictors of missed jobs (battery at start, open requests, overdue maintenance, repeat failures, calibration age) and test them on our data; do not borrow thresholds from other companies.** Rows 2, 4, 12, 15.

## 4. Source quality notes

- Press releases or company blogs: rows 2, 4, 8, 14, 15 (partly), 16. Treat as claims.
- Trade or general press: rows 3, 5, 6, 7, 11, 12.
- Vendor documentation: row 17. Standards bodies: rows 18, 19.
- Pages read directly: Waymo Fleet Response, Agility Arc release, Figure BMW post, Robot Report (Agility/GXO), Robot Report (Serve), Engadget, TechCrunch (Waymo), Gizmodo. The Orbit PDF fetch returned binary and the Starship blog returned 403, so those rows rely on search summaries.
- Nothing here has been independently audited. No staffing ratios, uptime values or cost figures were invented.
